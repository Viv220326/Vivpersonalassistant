# The Black Pearl — Brand Guidelines

## Overview

- **Category**: Premium / Fine Dining (Chinese)
- **Positioning**: Prestigious, high-end dining experience
- **Execution**: Internal + PR agency (PR handled by agency)

---

## Target Audience

| Segment | Profile |
|---|---|
| Affluent diners | Ages 25–50, high disposable income |
| Corporate / business | Private dining, client entertainment |
| Special occasions | Anniversaries, milestones, celebrations |
| High-spend locals & tourists | Destination dining, curated experience seekers |

---

## Tone of Voice

**Use:**
- Refined, elegant, confident
- Understated luxury — let quality speak
- Precise and authoritative
- Culturally rooted, modern sensibility

**Avoid:**
- Loud or salesy language
- Excessive exclamation marks
- Casual or overly playful phrasing
- Discounting or promotional desperation

---

## Brand Personality

- Sophisticated and composed
- Culturally grounded in Chinese culinary tradition
- Modern in execution without losing heritage

---

## Key Messages

1. **Culinary craftsmanship** — mastery in every detail
2. **Prestige & exclusivity** — an experience reserved for the discerning
3. **Elevated dining** — beyond a meal, a defining occasion

---

## Caption Formula (The Black Pearl)

- **Hook**: Evocative, not clickbait. Draw the reader in with atmosphere or craft.
- **Body**: 1–2 lines. Precise and evocative. No padding.
- **CTA**: Subtle. Invitation, not a push. (e.g. "Reserve your table." not "Book now!")

---

## Content Do's and Don'ts

| Do | Don't |
|---|---|
| Showcase plating and craft | Use low-quality or inconsistent imagery |
| Reference heritage and technique | Use generic food adjectives (delicious, yummy) |
| Highlight exclusivity subtly | Use countdown timers or urgency tactics |
| Reference seasons or curated menus | Over-explain the dish |
| Use white space in design | Clutter with logos, stickers, or text overlays |
