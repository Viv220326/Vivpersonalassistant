# Udon Shin Singapore — Brand Guidelines

## Overview

- **Category**: Casual Dining (Japanese Udon Concept)
- **Positioning**: Authentic, high-quality udon in a casual, accessible format
- **Execution**: Fully internal

---

## Target Audience

| Segment | Profile |
|---|---|
| Young professionals | Ages 20–40, lunch and dinner occasions |
| Japanese food enthusiasts | Appreciate authenticity and craft |
| Trend-aware diners | Follow food culture, value quality over hype |

---

## Tone of Voice

**Use:**
- Warm, approachable, and confident
- Casual but clean — not sloppy
- Lightly playful where appropriate
- Respectful of craft and heritage

**Avoid:**
- Overly formal or stiff language
- Trying too hard to be trendy or Gen Z coded
- Heavy emoji use
- Vague quality claims without substance

---

## Brand Personality

- Authentic and focused
- Minimalist — quality over noise
- Quietly confident about the craft

---

## Key Messages

1. **Handmade udon craftsmanship** — the process is the product
2. **Authentic Japanese experience** — no shortcuts, no compromises
3. **Simple, done right** — excellence in restraint

---

## Caption Formula (Udon Shin)

- **Hook**: Relatable or sensory. Pull them in with a moment or feeling.
- **Body**: Conversational but purposeful. 1–3 lines max.
- **CTA**: Clear and natural. (e.g. "See you at the counter." / "Link in bio to check the menu.")

---

## Content Do's and Don'ts

| Do | Don't |
|---|---|
| Feature the udon-making process | Use stock imagery or generic food shots |
| Highlight fresh, quality ingredients | Overclaim authenticity without evidence |
| Show the dining experience (texture, steam, pull) | Use clickbait or excessive urgency |
| Be specific about the dish or preparation | Use generic captions that could apply to any restaurant |
| Keep design clean and minimal | Clutter creative with too many elements |
